Facts from 3-lvgl that shape this milestone (verify, do not re-derive):

- Render: DIRECT mode into the 329,728-byte PSRAM canvas, one full-frame flush of about
  44 ms per refresh; touch-to-flush during a drag median 52 ms; main-loop poll gap under
  a continuous drag about 77 ms; refresh period 30 ms aligned with the touch poll; the
  indev runs in LV_INDEV_MODE_EVENT fed by pollInput. Thin primitives render intact.
- Memory: LVGL init costs about 32 KB of internal heap; idle heap about 113 KB on the
  playground build against about 150 KB on the current build; the ws-1.8 loop task
  needs the 16 KiB stack the sketch sets for the LVGL build (about 7.7 KB in use).
  Flash +279 KB (2.13 MB of 3 MB).
- Touch: LVGL hit-testing is exact (injected taps 24/24 on 24 px targets), but a finger
  plus the ~9 px calibration residual makes 24 px targets unreliable, 32 px marginal,
  44 px reliable, and worse near the edges. The saved calibration reaches roughly
  x=32–323 / y=28–415; a slider whose end sits at x=334 cannot reach its maximum, a
  flick that starts below y≈415 is dropped, and the panel's corner mask (about inset
  4 px / radius 50 px) clips the corners. The accepted layout stays within x=32–336 /
  y=28–416 with slider endpoints at x=52/316 for exactly this reason.
- BOOT: WaveshareButton.h already implements release-edge action, 30 ms debounce, no
  action for holds over 1 s, and the wake press consumed until a release; the normal
  build logs the press with "(no action)" and the playground cycles screens. `ui-nav
  next` performs the same action over USB without activity; `ui-button boot` injects the
  completed press.
- Screen switching: WaveshareLvgl::nextScreen cancels the active press with
  lv_indev_reset plus lv_indev_wait_release before lv_screen_load; the old widget
  receives INDEV_RESET (and drops its pressed look), the eventual release reaches no
  widget, and the new screen sees no phantom press. USB-verified.
- Threading rule (stated in WaveshareLvgl.h and docs/waveshare-frontend.md): the
  Arduino main task owns every LVGL call; worker and artwork tasks publish under their
  existing locks and the main task updates widgets from its copy. LVGL timers,
  animations, redraws, and injected input never touch DevicePower.
- Build: `--lvgl-playground` (.build/ws-1.8-lvgl) is a temporary evaluation toggle. This
  milestone makes LVGL the normal ws-1.8 build and retires the variant (one UI
  framework, no permanent second path); the touch-diagnostic variant stays.

==================================================
1. PRODUCT GOAL
==================================================

The controller needs room for richer interactions without making every touch target
tiny or relying on invisible global swipe gestures.

Introduce three conceptual screens:

  NOW PLAYING
  GROUPING
  PLAYGROUND

Initially:

NOW PLAYING
  existing useful playback UI

GROUPING
  placeholder / shell only in this milestone

PLAYGROUND
  dedicated area for interaction experiments

Rooms and Queue already exist as views reached from Now Playing (room header, Queue
button, Back). They remain SUB-VIEWS of Now Playing, not top-level screens:

  top-level:  Now Playing  ->  Grouping  ->  Playground  ->  Now Playing
  sub-views:  Now Playing { Rooms, Queue }

The navigation button cycles only top-level screens. Pressing it while a sub-view is
open moves to the next top-level screen and discards the sub-view; arriving back at Now
Playing later shows Now Playing itself, never a remembered sub-view.

Do not implement grouping functionality yet.

==================================================
2. HARDWARE-BUTTON SCREEN NAVIGATION
==================================================

Use BOOT (GPIO0) to cycle screens with ONE explicit short-press action:

  Now Playing
      ->
  Grouping
      ->
  Playground
      ->
  Now Playing

Reuse WaveshareButton.h unchanged: action on release, 30 ms debounce, holds longer than
1 s perform nothing, the press that woke or booted the device is consumed until a
release, and press/hold still counts as local activity exactly as today. Replace the
playground's `nextScreen` with the shell's top-level cycle; `ui-nav next` and
`ui-button boot` keep working and never count as local activity.

PWR stays unused: it is PMIC-owned, sensed through the expander, and a long press powers
the board off.

Do not use:

- global swipe navigation
- edge gestures
- hidden touch zones
- double/triple click navigation
- long-press navigation

Touch should remain local to the currently visible screen.

==================================================
3. SCREEN NAVIGATION STATE
==================================================

Model active screen separately from Sonos/application state.

Conceptually:

  ScreenNavigationState
    activeScreen

This is UI navigation state.

It is NOT:

- ObservedState
- PendingState
- Sonos state
- device config
- room state

Changing screens must never itself mutate Sonos.

Do not persist active screen across reboot/sleep.

Fresh boot starts at Now Playing.

Room change: WaveshareUi::update currently forces the Now Playing sub-view and cancels
any touch whenever the observed target changes. Keep that for sub-views, but a room
change must NOT change the top-level screen: a user on Playground stays on Playground.
Grouping is a placeholder and may do whatever is simplest.

==================================================
4. SCREEN-LOCAL INTERACTION
==================================================

Each screen may have ephemeral local interaction state.

When leaving a screen:

- cancel/release active touch interactions owned by that screen
- do not cancel already accepted Sonos mutations
- do not alter observed Sonos state
- do not alter grouping
- do not alter selected room

A touch started on one screen must never complete against controls on another screen.

A volume or seek preview active on Now Playing when the button is pressed is cancelled
and submits nothing.

==================================================
5. MINIMAL SCREEN INTERFACE
==================================================

Introduce only the smallest abstraction justified by multiple screens.

Conceptually something like:

  Screen
    onEnter()
    onLeave()
    handleTouch(...)
    render(...)

and:

  ScreenManager
    active screen
    next()

Exact names/design are implementation choices.

Each top-level screen is one LVGL screen object created once (at boot or lazily) and
switched with lv_screen_load; onEnter/onLeave are thin C++ hooks around the load. Before
switching, cancel the active touch exactly as WaveshareLvgl::nextScreen does today
(lv_indev_reset plus lv_indev_wait_release on the indev) so a press started on the old
screen cannot release on the new one. Now Playing's Rooms/Queue sub-views may be LVGL
containers toggled inside the Now Playing screen or separate LVGL screens; choose the
smaller change. No custom hit-testing.

Do not create:

- generic widget hierarchy
- virtual DOM
- animation framework
- layout engine
- navigation stack
- router

We need three screens and explicit lifecycle/ownership, not a UI framework.

==================================================
9. TOUCH OWNERSHIP
==================================================

Touch dispatch must go only to the active screen.

No screen should independently poll global touch state and accidentally react while
hidden.

A screen transition must clear/cancel active touch ownership.

LVGL already dispatches only to the loaded screen; the required proof is that a screen
switch during a press cannot complete on the new screen (section 5), re-verified over
USB after the shell replaces the playground's cycle.

Preserve current touch calibration in the hardware adapter: the calibrated (or injected)
sample stays the single indev path in Waveshare.cpp.

==================================================
10. POWER / ACTIVITY
==================================================

Physical screen-navigation presses count as local activity (already true: BOOT low sets
LocalActivity::Button).

Touch interactions on any screen count according to existing local activity semantics.

USB-injected navigation and touch do not count.

Changing screens does not otherwise alter sleep behavior. lvgl::stop() before the panel
powers down stays.

On wake/reboot:

  active screen = Now Playing

13. AUTONOMOUS DEVICE VERIFICATION
==================================================

Before reporting, prove the shell over USB on the identified ws-1.8 port (sleep disabled
for the session, profile restored afterward). Read the port continuously: the `usb`
helper discards device lines that arrive between one reply and the next command, which
hides LVGL event and frame lines (`.local/todo3/verify.ts` shows the pattern).

1. `ui-screen now|grouping|playground` each render; the `[lvgl] frame` or `[ui] frame`
   log reflects the change.
2. `ui-nav next` three times returns to Now Playing; the log shows each transition.
3. Inject a press on Now Playing's Play button, then `ui-nav next`, then a release:
   assert no intent was submitted and the new screen received no phantom release.
4. Inject a drag on the Playground control; assert its local value follows and no Sonos
   request appears in the log.
5. Inject a volume preview press, then `ui-nav next`; assert the preview is cancelled
   and nothing is submitted.
6. Open Rooms, then `ui-nav next`, then cycle back: assert Now Playing itself is shown.
7. Read the heartbeat heap and PSRAM before and after a few hundred injected
   navigations; the idle heap on the LVGL build is about 113 KB and the floor under a
   drag with an artwork download about 85 KB.
8. Confirm the touch-to-flush median stays about 52 ms and the poll gap under a drag
   under 100 ms with the accepted layout loaded.
